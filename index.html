How speech codecs work

A codec turns sound into bits and back, and speech codecs trade bit rate against quality. Here the encoder writes real bits. The channel can drop or flip them, and the decoder rebuilds the sound from whatever arrives.

The codecs

  • PCM rounds each sample to the nearest of a set of evenly spaced levels. Each bit adds about 6 dB of SNR. Telephone codecs sample at 8 kHz, so nothing above 3.4 kHz gets through.
  • G.711 μ-law and A-law use 8 bits, with fine steps for quiet sounds and coarse steps for loud ones, so quiet speech survives. They are the codecs of the phone network.
  • ADPCM (G.726, IMA) predicts each sample and sends only the error, with a step size that adapts. It needs about half G.711's bit rate.
  • Vocoders (LPC-10) don't send the waveform. Each frame carries the pitch, the loudness and a vocal-tract filter, and the decoder synthesises new speech. That's why the error trace is large even when the speech is clear. Digital radios such as DMR and P25 use vocoders from this family.

Packet loss and bit errors

A lost frame (shaded) is replaced by silence, by a repeat of the last frame, or by a repeat that fades. A bit error flips one bit at random, and each codec fails differently:

  • PCM clicks.
  • ADPCM drifts off, then recovers.
  • The vocoder garbles a whole frame.

Fixed random seeds mean the same settings always give the same damage.

The numbers

  • SNR compares the signal with the coding error over the whole clip.
  • Segmental SNR averages the SNR over 20 ms frames, each clamped to −10…35 dB, skipping near-silent frames. It tracks what you hear better than SNR does.
  • Spectral distortion is how far the shape of the spectrum differs, in dB. It is the one figure that also works for the vocoder.

All are measured at the codec rate, against the original band-limited the same way, so the lost bandwidth itself isn't counted.

The plots

Grey is the original, blue is what you hear and red is the error.

  • Codec detail: dots are the decoded samples. The overlay also shows the quantiser levels (PCM and G.711), the prediction and its step band (ADPCM), or the frames and pitch pulses (vocoder).
  • Waterfall: scrolls live, with the original on the left and the decoded sound on the right. Yellow is loud and purple is quiet. Change a setting while playing to see before and after.
